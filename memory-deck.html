The recommendation

Start with a question that has a checkable answer.

A useful first case has a named source, a person who knows what a good answer looks like, and a consequence worth checking.

Describe where context is missing, stale or difficult to retrieve. A memory stack is one possible response; the first change may be to improve the source itself.

Illustrative records and answer. This is a proposed workflow, not a live system demonstration.

The evidence

Keep the sample and scoring rule with the result.

The questions that matter

Choose tasks your team must answer correctly. Include exact identifiers, changing policies, conflicting records and missing information.

A reproducible check

Keep the source revision, expected evidence, scoring rule, system version and date with the result.

A bounded conclusion

Report the sample and the failure cases. A good score on selected internal questions is not a universal accuracy promise.

The earlier handbook described a targeted internal evaluation. Without its verified rubric and current run, this edition does not use that score as a production claim. For your workflow, agree the evidence that would justify a release.

The operator

Decide who maintains the evidence.

Assign an owner who can inspect sources, correct obsolete guidance and decide when the workflow should stop. Teach that person on real tasks and verify the agreed operating checks before handover.

What your team operates

Source updates, review of important answers, exception routing and the agreed quality checks. The exact tasks depend on the workflow.

What the agreement defines

Delivery work, ongoing support, record exports and software licences. Confirm who handles failures and which dependencies continue after handover.
