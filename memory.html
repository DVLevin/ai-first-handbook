The technical choices behind the trace.

Formation and retrieval are different jobs

Formation decides what becomes a durable record, with its origin, scope and review state. Retrieval decides which existing evidence is relevant to one question. A retrieved item should not become an accepted fact merely because an AI repeated it.

Exact identifiers and semantic meaning

An exact product ID or account reference needs a matching strategy that preserves the identifier. A question phrased differently from its source may need semantic retrieval. Test both kinds of need. No one retrieval technique is a substitute for an evaluation on your material.

Contradictions, supersession and freshness

Keep the author or source, revision and effective context with the record. When records disagree, define the authority rule and a route to a person. An old record can remain useful history while being unsuitable for a current answer.

The example above marks its old policy as superseded manually. It does not imply a verified automatic detector or a decay policy running in production.

Access, cost and maintenance

Check who may read each source and whether generated outputs can reveal it. A useful memory workflow also needs source maintenance, evaluation runs, monitoring and exception handling. Storage and model calls are only part of its cost.

Choose export and fallback requirements early. Keep client records, provider services and licensed supplier tooling explicit in the agreement.
